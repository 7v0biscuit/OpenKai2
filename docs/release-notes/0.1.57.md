## Budokai 3 textures

A real bug behind the black and blue Budokai 3 textures, found from your
test: every version that repacks the Budokai 3 textures was black, including
the normal import, while a plain Budokai 2 copy drew right.

Each texture's pixels and palette come after 32 bytes that send them to the
console's video memory, and the texture's header gives the size without
those 32 bytes. The import tool cut each piece at that size, so it lost the
last 32 bytes of every texture and every palette: half the colours of a
16-colour palette came out black or as stray data. The tool now copies all
of it.

- **Normal imports** of Budokai 3 characters (the textures on a Budokai 2
  body) should now show their colours. Delete the mods made with earlier
  versions and import them again.
- **Compare 4 versions (test)** now adds, in order:
  - **D**: the normal import, with the fix;
  - **H**: D, keeping the Budokai 2 character's own shading textures;
  - **J**: the whole Budokai 3 model with the Budokai 2 character's shading
    textures;
  - **A**: the whole Budokai 3 model, as **Force a new character** uses it.
  Please say which show colours. Forced imports were never repacked, so this
  bug wasn't what turned them black; J tests whether Budokai 3's own shading
  textures are.

Checked by unit tests, not yet in game.

## Music quality

Your startup log shows the music was produced at 98.8% of real time over the
run, but the game hitches for 40 to 100 ms every few seconds. The music
player kept an 85 ms buffer and slowed the music to about 80% whenever a
hitch drained it below 85%, then sped it back up: an audible warble at every
hitch. Once it had stretched once, it also kept every later sample going
through the time stretcher, which blends overlapping chunks even at normal
speed, until the music stopped.

- The buffer now holds 128 ms, so a 100 ms hitch plays through untouched.
- Stretching starts only when the buffer is low now and low on average over
  about a second, as PCSX2's and DuckStation's stretchers decide, so a single
  hitch never changes the tempo.
- While stretching, the tempo follows the measured rate with a faster trim,
  so it no longer overshoots and runs dry.
- Once the game keeps up again, playback hands back from the stretcher to the
  untouched music, crossfading at the seam.
- After a gap, the measured rate is kept, so a slow stream resumes stretched
  instead of running dry again.

The audio tests add a full-speed stream with a 100 ms stall every two seconds
(tempo must stay exactly 1, no gaps, the stretcher never used) and one that
runs at 80% and then recovers (it must return to untouched playback without
a gap). Music latency rises from about 85 ms to about 128 ms.
